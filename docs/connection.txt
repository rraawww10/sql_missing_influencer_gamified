WHAT YOU ASKED
   "Check whether all the episodes, levels and stages in the game story connect
    and sync with one another - the theme, the story and the clues. With proof."

WHAT I DID
   Read all 13 episode sources end to end (content/sessions/src/s01..s13.yaml,
   3,667 lines), the design doc (docs/SESSION_PLAN.md, 692 lines), the cast file
   (content/story/cast.json), and the 13 case-database snapshots. Then I did not
   trust the text: I ran every claim against the actual data with SQL, and wrote
   small scripts to check the clue graph, the hook-to-opening chain, the beat/unit
   coverage and the story clock. Every finding below has the query or the file
   and line number that proves it.

VERDICT IN ONE PARAGRAPH
   The spine is genuinely strong. All 19 clues form one backward-only chain with
   no dangling references, all 12 hooks land on the next episode's opening, the
   database grows table-by-table exactly as the story says it does, and work the
   learner does in one episode survives into the next (the alibi you type in
   Episode 2 is still there in Episode 6 and is used to catch the culprit). The
   numbers in the clue cards are real - 239/239 grader checks and 115/115 data
   checks pass, and the clue auditor flags 0 clue facts.

   BUT there are 3 real breaks and about 8 smaller ones. The most serious is a
   single sentence in Episode 12 that contradicts the learner's own query result.
   The other two are "story clock" problems: Episodes 3 and 6 show the learner
   data from hours in their own future, and Episode 6 openly calls a 23:28
   timestamp "right now" while the screen header says 8:30 PM.

   Nothing here is a code bug. Everything is content wording or data timing.

 BREAK 1  [SERIOUS]  Episode 12's clue contradicts the learner's own result
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE   content/sessions/src/s12.yaml:318   (beat S12.22, clue S12-C2)
        Also shipped in content/sessions/s12.json and missing-influencer-v3/.

THE TEXT
   "...user darora wrote Applicable at 18:05 (T7 - lost in the 18:07 crash)
    and again at 18:20 (T9 - committed...).
    >>> Both writes came AFTER she left the hotel in his SUV at 18:12 (S3-C1). <<<
    She never agreed to skip the show."

THE PROOF  (SELECT * FROM txn_log ORDER BY lsn, on the s12 snapshot)
   lsn 4   T7  START  darora  2026-12-12 18:05:10
   lsn 5   T7  WRITE  contract_C12.missed_show_clause  Not Applicable -> Applicable
                                         darora  2026-12-12 18:05:40   <<< 18:05
   lsn 6       CRASH  system          2026-12-12 18:07:00
   lsn 10  T9  START  darora          2026-12-12 18:20:05
   lsn 11  T9  WRITE  ...missed_show_clause   darora  18:20:30
   lsn 12  T9  WRITE  ...manager_bonus 0 -> 2500000   darora  18:20:40
   lsn 13  T9  COMMIT darora          18:21:00

WHY IT IS WRONG
   The first write is at 18:05:40. She left by the service gate at 18:12:05
   (S3-C1), her key card hit the service lift at 18:09:40 (S2-C1), and she
   cancelled her cab at 18:08:12 (S4-C1). So the T7 write came BEFORE all three.
   "Both writes came after" is false, and the same clue card states 18:05 two
   lines above - the card contradicts itself inside one paragraph.

WHY IT MATTERS MORE THAN A TYPO
   a) The learner has the log on screen (T12.9 returns those exact 7 rows). A
      sharp learner will see 18:05 and lose trust in the clue cards.
   b) It breaks the project's own evidence rule (SESSION_PLAN.md section 0.5:
      "states only what the result showed").
   c) The truth is actually a BETTER story. 18:05 is before she was taken - that
      is premeditation, not a cover-up. The current wording throws away the
      strongest fact in the episode.

NOTE  Two other places in the same episode get it right, which shows this is a
      slip, not a design decision:
        - S12.19b's explain (s12.yaml:291): "T7 WRITE by darora 18:05:40 ... CRASH
          18:07 ... T9 18:20:30" - correct.
        - the unit opener for RM #37: "edited by login darora at 18:20 - after
          Tara was already gone" - correct, because it only cites T9.
        - Episode 13's S13-C1 says "his committed contract change at 18:20" -
          correct.

SUGGESTED FIX (one sentence, no data change)
      "The first attempt came minutes BEFORE she left the hotel at 18:12 (S3-C1);
       the committed one came eight minutes after. She never agreed to skip the show."

 BREAK 2  [SERIOUS]  Episode 6 calls 23:28 "right now" - the header says 8:30 PM
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE   content/sessions/src/s06.yaml:63, :66, :73   (S6.4b and clue S6-C1)

4.1  THE EPISODE 10 REVEAL IS PRE-SPOILED
     Episode 10's clue S10-C1 is written as the reveal: "The car she left in is
     her MANAGER'S." But by then the learner already knows:
        Ep3  S3-C1: "the guard wrote the driver's name: Dev Arora" (he drove the
             SUV back at 19:52)
        Ep3  S3.20 reply: owner is "DA Talent Management Pvt Ltd" - and the
             initials DA are not subtle
        Ep6  S6-C2: Dev's phone was at the farm tower, breaking his alibi
     So Episode 10's two joins confirm something settled four episodes earlier.
     Mechanically it is a good JOIN lesson; dramatically it is a flat beat.
     If you want the reveal to land, Episode 3's guard could write an illegible
     or partial name, and the gate_log `person_name` for row 197 could be left
     NULL until the joins fill it in.

4.2  THE FINALE DOES NOT PROVE THE BEST CLUE
     Episode 13 asks for five claims: the SUV's owner, its round trip, the tower,
     the farm booking, and the contract edit. The Rs 1 "gReEn aCrEs" message -
     the one clue the VICTIM created, and the emotional centre of the story
     (S5-C1 and S7-C1) - is not one of the five. It is only recapped in Tara's
     opening dialogue. The learner never re-proves the clue that actually found her.

4.3  `person` AND `people` ARE THE SAME TWELVE PEOPLE
     From Episode 10 on, both tables exist with the SAME ids and the SAME names:
        person : (1,'Tara Sen'),(2,'Dev Arora'),(3,'Nysa Kapoor'),...,(12,'Pia Iyer')
        people : (1,'Tara Sen','missing'),(2,'Dev Arora','suspect'),...
     Episode 11 then uses `people` in T11.1/T11.3 and `person` in T11.4, with no
     line of dialogue explaining why there are two. Add `suspects` (Episode 2)
     and that is three overlapping people lists on screen at once.
     Also: the learner only ever INSERTS Dev into `person` (T9.5), but the s10
     snapshot has all 12 rows - the table is silently back-filled between
     episodes with no story beat to cover it.
